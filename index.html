✳  SI-ASSISTED SYNTHESIS / EVIDENCE-LED TAKEAWAY

Design for the fire you can’t predict from Earth.

Microgravity removes buoyant flow, changing flame shape and transport; partial gravity can also alter flammability. Together, SoFIE’s realistic atmosphere tests and Saffire’s larger-scale flame-spread work point to a practical priority: qualify materials and suppression strategies across pressure, oxygen, flow, and gravity conditions—not with a single terrestrial pass/fail.

Three experiments, three safety questions

NASA DATA
SAFFIRE / How did solid fuels spread in a spacecraft?Download data ↓
NASA reports Saffire-I SIBAL fabric (40.6 cm wide, 20 cm/s flow) spread rates of 1.8 mm/s in concurrent flow and 1.3 mm/s in opposed flow. Later Saffire tests varied sample width, material, and configuration. Keep each result in its test context; do not treat the combined records as a controlled material ranking. NASA NTRS · Saffire I–II ↗ · NASA NTRS · Saffire IV–V ↗
FLEX / When does a droplet flame go out?Download data ↓
For oxygen/nitrogen mixtures, NASA-supported ISS droplet experiments reported limiting oxygen indices of 0.12 for methanol and 0.15 for n-heptane. With CO₂ added to simulate an inert suppressant, the reported values rose to about 0.14 and 0.17 respectively. Large heptane droplets could continue quasi-steady cool-flame burning after the visible hot flame disappeared. These are specific droplet and atmosphere conditions, not universal cabin oxygen limits. NASA NTRS · FLEX paper ↗
SAME / Will the detector notice smoke from overheated materials?Download data ↓
In the tested conditions, the ISS light-scattering detector would not have alarmed in over half of the Kapton tests and two-thirds of the Teflon tests. Both are used in spacecraft electrical systems. This is a serious detection-design signal from a limited material/test set, not a quantified probability of a real onboard fire going undetected. Open study ↗ · NASA PSI-102 / SAME ↗
MISSION IMPLICATIONMaterial qualification, fire suppression, and smoke detection are connected but distinct design problems. No single metric from Saffire, FLEX, or SAME is enough to set a spacecraft safety rule; use the experiment conditions and limitations alongside every result.
